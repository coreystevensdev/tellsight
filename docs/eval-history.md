# Eval measurement history

Moved out of the README, which had reached 1,708 words in this subsection alone
and buried the parts a reader needs first. The README keeps what the harness
grades and which track gates CI; this file keeps how the numbers moved and
every time a published figure turned out to be wrong.

It is in this order on purpose. Read top to bottom and the pattern is that the
first reading of almost every measurement here was too confident, and the
correction came from raising the sample count rather than from changing the
system.

## The short version

| Date | What changed | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-09-08 | ~70 samples, v1.x | Faithfulness 0.96 to 0.99, legal posture 1 in 35 |
| 2026-09-10 | 155 samples, v2.0 | Faithfulness 0.99, all three floors held |
| 2026-10-31 | 10 samples per fixture, paired | Breached the faithfulness floor on one fixture, both arms |
| 2026-10-31 | 30 samples, same fixture | 0.851 against a 0.850 floor. The breach was sampling noise |
| 2026-11-01 | v2.1, four arms of 30 | Two of three changes measure as nothing, the third is the whole effect |
| 2026-11-04 | 30 per fixture, third judge | 0.943 and 0.921. The self-graded arm's advantage changes sign |

The last row is the one worth reading twice. An arm that had looked better was
being judged by the model under test, and with a third judge the difference
reverses and is noise in either direction.

## The measurements

**What it measured, and the first reading that was wrong.** The two older measurements here were taken on `claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929`, which is what `CLAUDE_MODEL` resolved to on the dates given. Measured across roughly 70 sampled generations on 2026-09-08, rather than one run, because the per-run spread is wider than the differences worth reporting: faithfulness lands between 0.96 and 0.99 aggregate against a floor of 0.85, completeness at 1.00, and legal posture fails about one sample in thirty-five. A further 155 samples on the deployed v2.0 prompt on 2026-09-10 held all three: faithfulness 0.99 aggregate with a 0.83 worst case, completeness 1.00, legal posture clean on every sample. A paired re-measurement on 2026-10-31, 10 samples per fixture on the same v2.0 prompt with the judge held at `claude-sonnet-5` in both arms, does not reproduce those numbers and breaches the faithfulness floor on one fixture in both arms: on `claude-sonnet-4-6` healthy-growth scored 0.859, cash-crunch 0.912 and seasonal-anomaly 0.794, aggregate 0.855; on `claude-sonnet-5` the same three scored 0.941, 0.909 and 0.748, aggregate 0.866. Completeness was 1.00 everywhere and the insight judge missed 0 of 30 in both. That first reading was wrong and the correction is the useful part. Both of those numbers came from 10 samples per fixture, and seasonal-anomaly was re-run at 30 on the serving model: v2.0 scores 0.851, which clears the 0.85 floor by 0.001, where the 10-sample arm had read 0.794 and failed it. The n=10 estimate sat inside the n=30 interval the whole time, so the breach was sampling noise quoted as a finding, which is the same mistake the insight-rate limitation below already documents.

**The gate carries no margin.** What survives is worse than a breach and harder to see: v2.0 sits at 0.851 against a 0.850 floor with a per-sample standard deviation of 0.13, so the gate carries no margin and the identical configuration passes or fails on sample count alone, with 14 of those 30 samples individually under it.

**v2.1 lifted the mean, and the cause was elsewhere.** The v2.1 prompt lifts the mean to 0.899, a matched gain of 0.048 at Welch t=1.56 on 56 degrees of freedom, so the direction is consistent and the effect is not established. Read it as suppressing a symptom: the cause is that a trend stat reached the model with no dates at all while every stat beside it was dated, which made "revenue held up while marketing fell" unverifiable by construction rather than untrue, and the 0.96 to 0.99 band above does not reproduce on either model. Dating that stat, and correcting a year-over-year figure that reported a month five months ahead of the date the prompt gives as today, scores 0.867 across 30 samples with a 95% interval of 0.824 to 0.910, so it did not move the number: the interval sits on top of the 0.899 it replaced and 14 of 30 samples are still under the floor. What it did do is categorical. The rejection class it targeted, the model asserting that the marketing decline and the December spike shared a window the stats never stated, went from 9 occurrences in the earlier run to none, and the judge now rejects a different claim instead: that there is no revenue series over the window to compare the marketing decline against, which is true, because this fixture carries a December anomaly, a November year-over-year and a July projection and no revenue trend at all. Handing the model a window it can name appears to have made it readier to attempt the comparison it was already inclined to make.

**Which of the three changes actually moved it.** A third arm separated them by holding the dated rendering and reverting only the year-over-year values, and a fourth added the revenue series the judge kept asking for: across four arms of 30 samples each, the trend dates are worth 0.020 at a Welch t of 0.66, the year-over-year correction 0.012 at t of 0.37, and the revenue series 0.100 at t of 4.06. Two of the three changes measure as nothing and the third is the entire effect. With the series present the fixture scores 0.967 with a 95% interval of 0.945 to 0.989, which overlaps none of the others, and the samples under the floor fall from 14 to 1. The rejection reasons had said so all along: the model kept naming what it could not check, first the unstated window and then the missing series, and the two fixes that addressed the first moved the score not at all. Read the 0.967 with the fixture in mind rather than the model, because a fixture that now supports the comparison the model wanted to make is an easier fixture, and that is inseparable from any gain in the answer; completeness went the other way, 1.00 to 0.983, since a fifth stat is more to cover.

**Re-measured with a third judge, so neither subject grades itself.** The `claude-sonnet-5` arm also tripped the legal-posture scanner once in thirty samples, on "it changes how you should read every other number here", which instructs nobody about money and is a banned phrase regardless; the v2.1 prompt carries that exact sentence as a worked counter-example. Read that arm's slightly higher faithfulness with suspicion rather than satisfaction, because it was judged by itself, which is the configuration the subject-model test now forbids. Re-run on 2026-11-04 with the judge overridden to `claude-opus-5` so neither subject grades itself, 30 samples per fixture on v2.1: `claude-sonnet-4-6` scores 0.924 on healthy-growth, 0.951 on cash-crunch and 0.954 on seasonal-anomaly, 0.943 aggregate across 87 samples, and `claude-sonnet-5` scores 0.902, 0.897 and 0.964, 0.921 across 89. Completeness is 1.00 in both, legal posture clean in both, and the insight judge misses none. The difference runs 0.022 the other way at 1.10 standard errors, a 95% interval of -0.060 to +0.017, so the self-graded arm's apparent advantage does not merely fail to reproduce, it changes sign and is noise in either direction. Nothing in it argues for changing the model production serves, and the cost case is thinner than the list price suggests: `claude-sonnet-5` bills $2/$10 against $3/$15 but uses the post-4.7 tokenizer, which produces roughly 30% more tokens for the same text, so the effective saving is nearer 13% than 33%. Read these two figures beside the earlier ones rather than as a continuation of them. The judge is different, and the seasonal-anomaly fixture now carries the revenue series it lacked, which accounts for most of the distance from 0.851 to the nineties.

**Legal posture: the one flag that was a false positive.** Legal posture flagged one of nine cash-crunch samples for the literal phrase "you need to," and that was read at the time as a genuine slip past the prompt. It was not. The sentence was "the gap between what you're earning and what you need to break even," which names a figure rather than telling anyone to do anything, and a substring match cannot separate the two. The matcher now exempts that phrase after a relative pronoun and the eval calls the same matcher production does, rather than rebuilding the regex from the shared list and drifting from it. That check is deterministic and runs offline here at QA time; the live SSE stream now runs the same check at request time too (see Known Limitations for why flagging, not blocking, is the ceiling for a streamed response).
